Pages link to each other, Quick Add shows what it read, and long pages are fast.

- **Page links.** Type `[[` and part of a title to link a page. The link follows the page through a rename and into a Markdown export, and every page shows what links to it.
- **Quick Add shows what it read.** The date, priority, tag, and folder light up where you typed them. A title typed on a calendar block reads the same way, so "Standup #work every weekday" tags and repeats it.
- **Long pages are fast.** A years-long journal opens at once, and typing and Find keep up.
- **Back and forward** through the pages you opened, with Cmd+[ and Cmd+].

### Pages and editing

- **Export one page** as Markdown from its menu, the command palette, or Cmd+Shift+E.
- **Code blocks have a language.** Pick one and the code is coloured for it. Paste code in and Pikos guesses.
- **Find matches a word** even when part of it is bold or italic.
- **Quitting keeps your last words.** Cmd+Q saves what you just typed before the app closes.
- **A failed save says so.** "Not saved" appears beside the word count, and clicking it tries again.
- **No white flash** when a page opens in dark mode.

### Keyboard and lists

- **Escape leaves the editor.** The page stays open, Space completes it, and the arrows move between pages. A second Escape goes to the calendar.
- **Shift-click selects what you see** between the two clicks, Completed included.
- **After deleting the open page**, the arrows carry on from where it was.
- **Deleting a repeating page from the list** asks whether to skip one date or delete them all.
- **One undo for a multi-page delete**, and emptying the trash asks once instead of making you type "delete".
- **A page you add in Inbox or Upcoming** stays in that view.
- **The command palette ranks better.** A command whose word starts with what you typed comes first, with the letters marked.

### Quick Add and dates

- **"Morning pages tomorrow 9am"** keeps "Morning pages" as the title.
- **"Next week"** means the coming Monday.
- **An all-day page due today reads "Today"** in amber.

### Calendar

- **A denser setting.** The calendar goes one step tighter, so more of your day fits on screen, and short events are drawn at their real length.
- **Multi-day events in month view** draw as one bar per week.
- **Events draw with the grid** when you open the calendar, instead of popping in after it.
- **The current-time line is where it should be** when you come back to Pikos from another app.
- **A block's time that doesn't fit** ends in "…" instead of being cut mid-letter.
- **The all-day strip grows** with the calendar's text size.
- **A meeting's agenda arrives as a bullet list**, links and all.
- **A focus session running when you quit** is recorded with its real end time.

### Look and safety

- **Easier to read.** Text, links, hints, and buttons meet the contrast bar in both themes, and a priority is the same colour everywhere. Red folders are now rose.
- **A damaged workspace says so** and offers to restore a backup, instead of failing quietly.
- **Reminders macOS blocks aren't lost quietly.** Recent notifications marks them as not shown, and Settings has a button to System Settings to turn them back on.
- **A Markdown import names any image it couldn't find.**

### Privacy

- **Deleting a page for good deletes its notification history** too.
- **An image no page uses any more** is removed from your Mac at the next launch. A page in the trash or a backup keeps its images.

### Command line and MCP

- **Edit one section of a page** by its heading: add to it, insert under it, or replace it, leaving the rest untouched.
- **Your typing and an outside edit both survive.** If the command line, an assistant, or another window changes the page you're typing in, Pikos merges the two instead of keeping only yours.
- **An open page shows outside changes.** When the command line or an assistant changes a page you have open and haven't touched, the new text appears in place.

0.5.0 upgrades your workspace and 0.4.1 can't open it afterwards. Pikos backs up the old one first.
